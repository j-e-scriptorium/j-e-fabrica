# Sources wanted

Works not yet available to the build that would most improve the data, grouped by the weakness each would address (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md` numbers in brackets). A PDF or full-text copy of any of them would let records be added or rechecked at tier A rather than recalled. Bibliographic details are from general knowledge and should be checked against a catalogue.

Already read in full and in the record: *Handbook of North American Indians* vols. 10 (Jicarilla), 11 (Eastern Shoshone) and 13; the 1890 census *Report on Indians Taxed and Indians Not Taxed* (1894); Hämäläinen, *The Comanche Empire* (2008).

## 1. The Comanche, 1819–1875 [1]

1. Thomas W. Kavanagh, *Comanche Political History: An Ethnohistorical Perspective, 1706–1875* (1996). The single most useful work still missing: it would say how each figure of 1819–49 was made and what part of the nation it covers, which is the whole of the open question.
2. Dorman H. Winfrey and James M. Day, eds., *The Indian Papers of Texas and the Southwest, 1825–1916* (1959–66): the Butler and Lewis (1846), Neighbors (1849) and Burnet letters themselves; also the Tonkawa and Kitsai counts of 1849–63, now known only through the Handbook.

## 2. Population elsewhere

3. *Annual Reports of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs*, the statistical tables for every year 1849–1900. Fewer than a dozen years are in the record; the rest would turn the reservation era into an annual series.
4. Canada, *Annual Reports of the Department of Indian Affairs*, 1880–1900 (Sessional Papers), and the Treaty 4, 6 and 7 annuity paylists [4]: the Plains Cree, Plains Ojibwe, Northern Assiniboine, Sarsi, Stoney and Blackfoot reserves.
5. The 1900 U.S. census Indian schedules or their published summary, enumerated household by household: the only independent test of the agency rolls [5].
6. Kingsley M. Bray, "Teton Sioux Population History, 1655–1881," *Nebraska History* 75 (1994). A division-level reconstruction directly comparable with the Lakota fit.
7. John H. Moore, *The Cheyenne Nation: A Social and Demographic History* (1987). Cited from recall only; its demographic chapters bear on the Cheyenne series and the 1849 cholera.
8. Douglas H. Ubelaker, "North American Indian Population Size: Changing Perspectives," *Handbook of North American Indians*, vol. 3 (2006), with his tribe-by-tribe revision of Mooney's 1780 figures.

## 3. Epidemics [2]

9. Elizabeth A. Fenn, *Pox Americana: The Great Smallpox Epidemic of 1775–82* (2001).
10. Russell Thornton, *American Indian Holocaust and Survival* (1987).
11. Michael K. Trimble, *An Ethnohistorical Interpretation of the Spread of Smallpox in the Northern Plains Utilizing Concepts of Disease Ecology* (Reprints in Anthropology 33, 1986), and Annie Heloise Abel, ed., *Chardon's Journal at Fort Clark, 1834–1839* (1932), for 1837.
12. Paul Hackett, *"A Very Remarkable Sickness": Epidemics in the Petit Nord, 1670 to 1846* (2002), for the Cree, Assiniboine and Stoney.
13. R. G. Robertson, *Rotting Face: Smallpox and the American Indian* (2001).

## 4. The eighteenth century [3]

14. Dale R. Russell, *Eighteenth-Century Western Cree and Their Neighbours* (1991).
15. Lawrence J. Burpee, ed., "York Factory to the Blackfeet Country: The Journal of Anthony Hendry, 1754–55" (*Transactions of the Royal Society of Canada*, 1907); "An Adventurer from Hudson Bay: Journal of Matthew Cocking, 1772–73" (1908); and *Journals and Letters of Pierre Gaultier de Varennes de la Vérendrye and His Sons* (Champlain Society, 1927). Henday and Cocking are also the first observers of the Sarsi and Stoney.
16. Alfred B. Thomas, *After Coronado* (1935), *Forgotten Frontiers* (1932) and *The Plains Indians and New Mexico, 1751–1778* (1940): the Spanish documents for the Apache, Comanche and Jicarilla.
17. A. P. Nasatir, ed., *Before Lewis and Clark: Documents Illustrating the History of the Missouri, 1785–1804* (1952), for Truteau and Mackay.
18. Elizabeth A. H. John, *Storms Brewed in Other Men's Worlds* (1975), and Herbert E. Bolton, ed., *Athanase de Mézières and the Louisiana–Texas Frontier, 1768–1780* (1914), for Wichita, Kitsai, Tonkawa, Comanche and Lipan counts.
19. W. Raymond Wood and Thomas D. Thiessen, eds., *Early Fur Trade on the Northern Plains: Canadian Traders among the Mandan and Hidatsa Indians, 1738–1818* (1985).
20. Theodore Binnema, *Common and Contested Ground: A Human and Environmental History of the Northwestern Plains* (2001).

## 5. Positions and seasonal rounds [9]

21. *Historical Atlas of Canada*, vol. 1, *From the Beginning to 1800* (1987), the plates on the western interior and the fur trade.
22. Helen Hornbeck Tanner, ed., *Atlas of Great Lakes Indian History* (1987), for the Dakota, Ojibwe and Iowa.
23. D. W. Moodie and Arthur J. Ray, "Buffalo Migrations in the Canadian Plains," *Plains Anthropologist* 21 (1976); John C. Ewers, *The Horse in Blackfoot Indian Culture* (1955); Elliott West, *The Contested Plains* (1998). All three are behind seasonal patterns that were recalled, not reread.

## 6. Bands and the new peoples [6]

24. Edwin T. Denig, *Five Indian Tribes of the Upper Missouri*, ed. John C. Ewers (1961).
25. George E. Hyde, *Red Cloud's Folk* (1937) and *Spotted Tail's Folk* (1961); Catherine Price, *The Oglala People, 1841–1879* (1996).
26. Diamond Jenness, *The Sarcee Indians of Alberta* (1938), for Sarsi bands and camps; Thomas F. Schilz, *People of the Cross Timbers: A History of the Tonkawa Indians* (1987).
