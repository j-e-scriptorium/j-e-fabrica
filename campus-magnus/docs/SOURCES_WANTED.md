# Sources wanted

A request for copies (PDF scans or full text) of the works and documents below, for research on the population and locations of the Great Plains Indian peoples, 1700–1900. Where only part of a work is wanted, the part is named. Scans should show tables in full. Publication details are given from memory and should be checked against a catalogue.

## Comanche and Texas tribes, 1846–1865

1. John H. Stem, U.S. Indian agent for Texas, report on the Indians of Texas, 1851, with any count or estimate of the Comanche, Wichita, Waco, Tawakoni, Kitsai and Tonkawa it contains (National Archives, Record Group 75, Records of the Bureau of Indian Affairs, Letters Received, Texas Agency).
2. William J. Hardee, report on the Indians of Texas, 1852, with its estimate of the tribes (National Archives, War Department or Office of Indian Affairs records).
3. The rolls or censuses of the Brazos Agency (Caddo, Anadarko, Waco, Tawakoni, Tonkawa) and the Comanche Reserve on the Clear Fork of the Brazos (Penateka Comanche), 1855–1859 (National Archives, Record Group 75, Records of the Texas Agency and Letters Received, Texas Agency).

## Population tables, United States

4. *Annual Report of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs*, the statistical table of Indian population by agency and tribe, all pages of it, for each of the years 1849, 1850, 1851, 1852, 1853, 1856, 1857, 1858, 1859, 1860, 1865, 1871, 1872, 1879, 1881, 1889, 1890, 1893 and 1899.
5. U.S. Census Office, Twelfth Census (1900): the published summary of the Indian population by tribe and reservation, or the Indian schedules for the reservations of the Dakotas, Montana, Wyoming, Nebraska, Kansas and Oklahoma.
6. John H. Moore, *The Cheyenne Nation: A Social and Demographic History* (Lincoln: University of Nebraska Press, 1987): the demographic chapters, and any discussion of the cholera of 1849.
7. Douglas H. Ubelaker, "North American Indian Population Size: Changing Perspectives," in *Handbook of North American Indians*, vol. 3, *Environment, Origins, and Population* (Washington: Smithsonian Institution, 2006), with its tribe-by-tribe table revising James Mooney's estimates for about 1780.
8. The report of J. W. Daniels, agent at Red Cloud Agency, in the *Annual Report of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs* for 1872: which of the two figures, 1,342 and 1,515, belongs to the Northern Cheyenne and which to the Northern Arapaho (the secondary works assign them both ways).

## Population tables, Canada

9. Canada, Department of Indian Affairs, *Annual Report*, in the Sessional Papers, for the years 1881, 1882, 1885, 1889, 1891, 1894, 1895, 1896 and 1897: the tabular statements of population by band and reserve for the North-West Territories (Treaties 4, 6 and 7), at the level of the individual band rather than the treaty total.
10. The annuity paylists for Treaty 4 (1874), Treaty 6 (1876) and Treaty 7 (1877), 1874–1900, by band: the Plains Cree, Plains Ojibwe (Saulteaux), Assiniboine, Sarcee (Tsuu T'ina), Stoney and Blackfoot (Siksika, Kainai, Piikani) reserves (Library and Archives Canada, RG 10).

## Epidemics

11. Russell Thornton, *American Indian Holocaust and Survival: A Population History since 1492* (Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1987).
12. Michael K. Trimble, *An Ethnohistorical Interpretation of the Spread of Smallpox in the Northern Plains Utilizing Concepts of Disease Ecology* (Lincoln: J & L Reprint Co., Reprints in Anthropology 33, 1986).
13. Annie Heloise Abel, ed., *Chardon's Journal at Fort Clark, 1834–1839* (Pierre: South Dakota Department of History, 1932): the entries and notes for the smallpox of 1837–1838.
14. Paul Hackett, *"A Very Remarkable Sickness": Epidemics in the Petit Nord, 1670 to 1846* (Winnipeg: University of Manitoba Press, 2002).
15. R. G. Robertson, *Rotting Face: Smallpox and the American Indian* (Caldwell, Idaho: Caxton Press, 2001).

## The eighteenth century

16. Dale R. Russell, *Eighteenth-Century Western Cree and Their Neighbours* (Hull, Quebec: Canadian Museum of Civilization, Mercury Series, Archaeological Survey of Canada Paper 143, 1991).
17. Any reconstruction of Anthony Henday's route of 1754–1755 published after 1939, with its identification of the "Archithinue" he met in October 1754.
18. Hudson's Bay Company post journals, 1770–1800, for the Saskatchewan posts (Cumberland House, Hudson House, Manchester House, South Branch House, Fort George, Buckingham House, Edmonton House), the entries naming the Indian peoples who came to trade (Hudson's Bay Company Archives, Archives of Manitoba, series B).
19. Alfred Barnaby Thomas, ed. and trans., *After Coronado: Spanish Exploration Northeast of New Mexico, 1696–1727* (Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1935).
20. Alfred Barnaby Thomas, ed. and trans., *Forgotten Frontiers: A Study of the Spanish Indian Policy of Don Juan Bautista de Anza, Governor of New Mexico, 1777–1787* (Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1932).
21. Alfred Barnaby Thomas, ed. and trans., *The Plains Indians and New Mexico, 1751–1778* (Albuquerque: University of New Mexico Press, 1940).
22. A. P. Nasatir, ed., *Before Lewis and Clark: Documents Illustrating the History of the Missouri, 1785–1804*, 2 vols. (St. Louis: St. Louis Historical Documents Foundation, 1952).
23. Elizabeth A. H. John, *Storms Brewed in Other Men's Worlds: The Confrontation of Indians, Spanish, and French in the Southwest, 1540–1795* (College Station: Texas A&M University Press, 1975).
24. W. Raymond Wood and Thomas D. Thiessen, eds., *Early Fur Trade on the Northern Plains: Canadian Traders among the Mandan and Hidatsa Indians, 1738–1818* (Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1985).

## Locations and seasonal movements

25. R. Cole Harris, ed., *Historical Atlas of Canada*, vol. 1, *From the Beginning to 1800* (Toronto: University of Toronto Press, 1987): the plates on the western interior and the fur trade, with their notes.
26. Helen Hornbeck Tanner, ed., *Atlas of Great Lakes Indian History* (Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1987): the maps and text for the Dakota, Ojibwe and Iowa.
27. D. W. Moodie and Arthur J. Ray, "Buffalo Migrations in the Canadian Plains," *Plains Anthropologist* 21, no. 71 (1976): the whole article.
28. Arthur J. Ray, *Indians in the Fur Trade: Their Role as Trappers, Hunters, and Middlemen in the Lands Southwest of Hudson Bay, 1660–1870* (Toronto: University of Toronto Press, 1974): the chapters on the seasonal movements of the Cree and Assiniboine.
29. Frederick E. Hoxie, *Parading through History: The Making of the Crow Nation in America, 1805–1935* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1995): the chapters on the Crow before the reservation.

## Bands and divisions

30. George E. Hyde, *Spotted Tail's Folk: A History of the Brulé Sioux* (Norman: University of Oklahoma Press, 1961).
31. Catherine Price, *The Oglala People, 1841–1879: A Political History* (Lincoln: University of Nebraska Press, 1996).
32. Thomas F. Schilz, *People of the Cross Timbers: A History of the Tonkawa Indians* (Fort Worth: TCU Press, 1987).
