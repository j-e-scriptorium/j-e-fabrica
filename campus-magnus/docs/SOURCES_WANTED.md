# Sources wanted

Works not yet available to the build that would most improve the data, grouped by the weakness each would address (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md` numbers in brackets). A PDF or full-text copy of any of them would let records be added or rechecked at tier A rather than recalled. Bibliographic details are from general knowledge and should be checked against a catalogue.

Already read in full and in the record: *Handbook of North American Indians* vols. 10 (Jicarilla), 11 (Eastern Shoshone) and 13; the 1890 census *Report on Indians Taxed and Indians Not Taxed* (1894); Hämäläinen, *The Comanche Empire* (2008); Kavanagh, *Comanche Political History* (1996); Fenn, *Pox Americana* (2001).

## 1. The Comanche, 1846–1865 [1]

1. Dorman H. Winfrey and James M. Day, eds., *The Indian Papers of Texas and the Southwest, 1825–1916* (1959–66): the Neighbors (1849), Stem (1851) and Hardee (1852) reports in full; also the Tonkawa and Kitsai counts of 1849–63, known here only through the Handbook.

## 2. Population elsewhere

2. *Annual Reports of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs*, the statistical tables for every year 1849–1900. Fewer than a dozen years are in the record; the rest would turn the reservation era into an annual series.
3. Canada, *Annual Reports of the Department of Indian Affairs*, 1880–1900 (Sessional Papers), and the Treaty 4, 6 and 7 annuity paylists [4]: the Plains Cree, Plains Ojibwe, Northern Assiniboine, Sarsi, Stoney and Blackfoot reserves.
4. The 1900 U.S. census Indian schedules or their published summary, enumerated household by household: the only independent test of the agency rolls [5].
5. Kingsley M. Bray, "Teton Sioux Population History, 1655–1881," *Nebraska History* 75 (1994). A division-level reconstruction directly comparable with the Lakota fit.
6. John H. Moore, *The Cheyenne Nation: A Social and Demographic History* (1987). Cited from recall only; its demographic chapters bear on the Cheyenne series and the 1849 cholera.
7. Douglas H. Ubelaker, "North American Indian Population Size: Changing Perspectives," *Handbook of North American Indians*, vol. 3 (2006), with his tribe-by-tribe revision of Mooney's 1780 figures.

## 3. Epidemics [2]

8. Russell Thornton, *American Indian Holocaust and Survival* (1987).
9. Michael K. Trimble, *An Ethnohistorical Interpretation of the Spread of Smallpox in the Northern Plains Utilizing Concepts of Disease Ecology* (Reprints in Anthropology 33, 1986), and Annie Heloise Abel, ed., *Chardon's Journal at Fort Clark, 1834–1839* (1932), for 1837.
10. Paul Hackett, *"A Very Remarkable Sickness": Epidemics in the Petit Nord, 1670 to 1846* (2002), for the Cree, Assiniboine and Stoney.
11. R. G. Robertson, *Rotting Face: Smallpox and the American Indian* (2001).

## 4. The eighteenth century [3]

12. Dale R. Russell, *Eighteenth-Century Western Cree and Their Neighbours* (1991).
13. Lawrence J. Burpee, ed., "York Factory to the Blackfeet Country: The Journal of Anthony Hendry, 1754–55" (*Transactions of the Royal Society of Canada*, 1907); "An Adventurer from Hudson Bay: Journal of Matthew Cocking, 1772–73" (1908); and *Journals and Letters of Pierre Gaultier de Varennes de la Vérendrye and His Sons* (Champlain Society, 1927). Henday and Cocking are also the first observers of the Sarsi and Stoney.
14. Alfred B. Thomas, *After Coronado* (1935), *Forgotten Frontiers* (1932) and *The Plains Indians and New Mexico, 1751–1778* (1940): the Spanish documents for the Apache, Comanche and Jicarilla.
15. A. P. Nasatir, ed., *Before Lewis and Clark: Documents Illustrating the History of the Missouri, 1785–1804* (1952), for Truteau and Mackay.
16. Elizabeth A. H. John, *Storms Brewed in Other Men's Worlds* (1975), and Herbert E. Bolton, ed., *Athanase de Mézières and the Louisiana–Texas Frontier, 1768–1780* (1914), for Wichita, Kitsai, Tonkawa, Comanche and Lipan counts.
17. W. Raymond Wood and Thomas D. Thiessen, eds., *Early Fur Trade on the Northern Plains: Canadian Traders among the Mandan and Hidatsa Indians, 1738–1818* (1985).
18. Theodore Binnema, *Common and Contested Ground: A Human and Environmental History of the Northwestern Plains* (2001).

## 5. Positions and seasonal rounds [9]

19. *Historical Atlas of Canada*, vol. 1, *From the Beginning to 1800* (1987), the plates on the western interior and the fur trade.
20. Helen Hornbeck Tanner, ed., *Atlas of Great Lakes Indian History* (1987), for the Dakota, Ojibwe and Iowa.
21. D. W. Moodie and Arthur J. Ray, "Buffalo Migrations in the Canadian Plains," *Plains Anthropologist* 21 (1976); John C. Ewers, *The Horse in Blackfoot Indian Culture* (1955); Elliott West, *The Contested Plains* (1998). All three are behind seasonal patterns that were recalled, not reread.

## 6. Bands and the new peoples [6]

22. Edwin T. Denig, *Five Indian Tribes of the Upper Missouri*, ed. John C. Ewers (1961).
23. George E. Hyde, *Red Cloud's Folk* (1937) and *Spotted Tail's Folk* (1961); Catherine Price, *The Oglala People, 1841–1879* (1996).
24. Diamond Jenness, *The Sarcee Indians of Alberta* (1938), for Sarsi bands and camps; Thomas F. Schilz, *People of the Cross Timbers: A History of the Tonkawa Indians* (1987).
