# Sources wanted

Works not available to the build that would most improve the data, grouped by the weakness each would address (`KNOWN_ISSUES.md` numbers in brackets). A PDF or full-text copy of any of them would let records be added or rechecked at tier A rather than recalled. Bibliographic details are from general knowledge and should be checked against a catalogue.

## 1. Peoples missing from the map

1. *Handbook of North American Indians*, vol. 13, *Plains* (2001), again, for the chapters not yet used: the Sarsi (Tsuut’ina), the Stoney (Mountain Assiniboine), the Tonkawa, the Kitsai and the Plains Métis. The Sarsi and Stoney are the clearest omissions from a map claiming every Plains people; the Tonkawa and Kitsai are on its southern margin. The same volume’s “Teton” would also give first-attestation dates for the seven Lakota divisions [10].
2. *Handbook of North American Indians*, vol. 11, *Great Basin* (1986), Shimkin, “Eastern Shoshone”; and vol. 10, *Southwest* (1983), Opler, “Jicarilla Apache”. The Shoshone and Jicarilla rest on other sources; neither has a page-cited Handbook chapter behind it.

## 2. Population

3. U.S. Census Office, *Report on Indians Taxed and Indians Not Taxed in the United States … at the Eleventh Census: 1890* (1894). Reservation-by-reservation counts for one fixed year, the best independent check on agency rolls [5].
4. *Annual Reports of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs*, the statistical tables for every year 1849–1900. Only eight years were read; the rest would turn the reservation era from anchors every decade into an annual series.
5. Canada, *Annual Reports of the Department of Indian Affairs*, 1880–1900 (Sessional Papers), and the Treaty 4 and 6 annuity paylists [4].
6. Kingsley M. Bray, “Teton Sioux Population History, 1655–1881,” *Nebraska History* 75 (1994). A division-level reconstruction directly comparable with the Lakota fit.
7. John H. Moore, *The Cheyenne Nation: A Social and Demographic History* (1987). Cited from recall only; its demographic chapters bear on the Cheyenne series and the 1849 cholera.
8. Douglas H. Ubelaker, “North American Indian Population Size: Changing Perspectives,” *Handbook of North American Indians*, vol. 3 (2006), with his tribe-by-tribe revision of Mooney’s 1780 figures.

## 3. The Comanche, 1840–1875 [1]

9. Pekka Hämäläinen, *The Comanche Empire* (2008), chs. 7–8 and their notes on the 1840s figures. Every Hämäläinen record is second-hand (tier C).
10. Thomas W. Kavanagh, *Comanche Political History: An Ethnohistorical Perspective, 1706–1875* (1996), for division-by-division counts.
11. Dorman H. Winfrey and James M. Day, eds., *The Indian Papers of Texas and the Southwest, 1825–1916* (1959–66), for the Butler and Lewis (1846) and Neighbors (1849) reports and how they were made.

A trial run for this review added an assumed 35–60% loss to the Comanche in 1855–65 (drought, bison failure, war). It raised the 1846 fit only from about 10,000 to 11,000–12,600, because the fit is held down less by the growth prior than by Hodge’s small division figures for 1869–72 (Yamparika 356 and 200, Nokoni 312 and 250), which lie well below the fit and probably count only those then at the agency. Whether those figures are partial counts or whole divisions is the question these works would settle.

## 4. Epidemics [2]

12. Elizabeth A. Fenn, *Pox Americana: The Great Smallpox Epidemic of 1775–82* (2001).
13. Russell Thornton, *American Indian Holocaust and Survival* (1987).
14. Michael K. Trimble, *An Ethnohistorical Interpretation of the Spread of Smallpox in the Northern Plains Utilizing Concepts of Disease Ecology* (Reprints in Anthropology 33, 1986), and Annie Heloise Abel, ed., *Chardon’s Journal at Fort Clark, 1834–1839* (1932), for 1837.
15. Paul Hackett, *“A Very Remarkable Sickness”: Epidemics in the Petit Nord, 1670 to 1846* (2002), for the Cree and Assiniboine.
16. R. G. Robertson, *Rotting Face: Smallpox and the American Indian* (2001).

## 5. The eighteenth century [3]

17. Dale R. Russell, *Eighteenth-Century Western Cree and Their Neighbours* (1991).
18. Lawrence J. Burpee, ed., “York Factory to the Blackfeet Country: The Journal of Anthony Hendry, 1754–55” (*Transactions of the Royal Society of Canada*, 1907); “An Adventurer from Hudson Bay: Journal of Matthew Cocking, 1772–73” (1908); and *Journals and Letters of Pierre Gaultier de Varennes de la Vérendrye and His Sons* (Champlain Society, 1927).
19. Alfred B. Thomas, *After Coronado* (1935), *Forgotten Frontiers* (1932) and *The Plains Indians and New Mexico, 1751–1778* (1940): the Spanish documents for the Apache, Comanche and Jicarilla.
20. A. P. Nasatir, ed., *Before Lewis and Clark: Documents Illustrating the History of the Missouri, 1785–1804* (1952), for Truteau and Mackay.
21. Elizabeth A. H. John, *Storms Brewed in Other Men’s Worlds: The Confrontation of Indians, Spanish, and French in the Southwest, 1540–1795* (1975), and Herbert E. Bolton, ed., *Athanase de Mézières and the Louisiana–Texas Frontier, 1768–1780* (1914), for Wichita, Comanche and Lipan counts.
22. W. Raymond Wood and Thomas D. Thiessen, eds., *Early Fur Trade on the Northern Plains: Canadian Traders among the Mandan and Hidatsa Indians, 1738–1818* (1985).
23. Theodore Binnema, *Common and Contested Ground: A Human and Environmental History of the Northwestern Plains* (2001).

## 6. Positions and seasonal rounds [9]

24. *Historical Atlas of Canada*, vol. 1, *From the Beginning to 1800* (1987), the plates on the western interior and the fur trade: dated band and post positions for the Cree, Assiniboine, Blackfoot and Gros Ventre.
25. Helen Hornbeck Tanner, ed., *Atlas of Great Lakes Indian History* (1987), for the eastern margin: Dakota, Ojibwe, Iowa.
26. D. W. Moodie and Arthur J. Ray, “Buffalo Migrations in the Canadian Plains,” *Plains Anthropologist* 21 (1976); John C. Ewers, *The Horse in Blackfoot Indian Culture* (1955); Elliott West, *The Contested Plains* (1998). All three are behind seasonal patterns that were recalled, not reread.

## 7. Bands [6]

27. Edwin T. Denig, *Five Indian Tribes of the Upper Missouri*, ed. John C. Ewers (1961): his Crow, Cree, Assiniboine, Sioux and Arikara chapters, fuller than the BAE 46 text.
28. George E. Hyde, *Red Cloud’s Folk* (1937) and *Spotted Tail’s Folk* (1961); Catherine Price, *The Oglala People, 1841–1879* (1996), for Lakota band histories.
