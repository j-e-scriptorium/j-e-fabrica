School

A mancala for the hand: stretch the string from peg to peg, and the bones it crosses are yours.

By Ludovico Ambrosius and S. Minerva Scribner.

The board is a ring of pegs with a scatter of "bones" — tokens — set out among them. On your turn you draw a straight line, a taut string, from one peg to another anywhere around the ring; every bone that line passes over is captured and added to your score. In the game's "full form," capturing costs something back: each turn you also return one tithe-bone to the water at the center of the board, so a run of easy captures thins out and the endgame tightens.

Play against a friend, or set either seat to a computer opponent of one of four strengths — Greedy, which always takes the biggest capture in front of it; One-ply and Two-ply, which look one or two moves ahead; and Two-ply with boxing-out, which also plays for position rather than points alone. The board itself is adjustable: how many pegs ring it, how many bones are in play, and how large the bones are drawn.
